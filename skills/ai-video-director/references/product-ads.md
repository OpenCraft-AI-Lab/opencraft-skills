# Product ads

The ad's subject is a particular product, not an interchangeable series of attractive shots. A viewer should be able to recognize it in the opening detail, the main reveal, and the final branded frame.

## Design the visual progression

Lock a product sheet: silhouette, materials, liquid or fill level, cap, label, surface finish, and any feature that must remain physically plausible. Check these traits in dark shots as well as hero shots. Define a “home pose” such as a front-facing, centered bottle. Use macro shots to reveal its signature detail, then return to the home pose so the viewer knows the close-up belongs to the same object.

Pick one world and light language: background, palette, direction of light travel, haze level, and camera movement. Build a reveal such as detail → partial product → full product → final packshot. The exact order can change with the concept, but consecutive shots need a visible connection. A shared object position and light state often joins better than a decorative transition effect. Create the next start frame from the previous shot's *actual used* last frame when continuity is crucial, then check the resulting clip at the intended cut. Keep the final brand mark with the product when that strengthens the packshot; leave enough clear space and time to read it on a phone.

Before adding more clips, compare the current cut with the acceptance test. If every shot has a different light setup, redesign the sequence around one visual progression. If an opener matches only the bottle's outline but lacks its materials and lighting, the shape match may still feel disconnected. If a transition lifts the intended blacks or changes the palette, inspect the raw cuts and other available footage first. Use faster playback only if it preserves plausible motion; a light sweep is easier to speed than a human hand or turning object.

Inspect the product during effects and ending animation: changing liquid level, a disappearing cap, an invented tube, red mist turning to fire, or a brand wordmark landing over the bottle can break the claim of a consistent product. Treat the example's observed fixes as options, not preset numbers. Read [the EP11 perfume example](examples/perfume-ad.md) for the decisions and their limits.
