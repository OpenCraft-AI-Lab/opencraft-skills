# AI short dramas

A short drama needs a cause, an action, and a consequence the viewer can read. Plan the ending before making a list of beautiful locations. Different story shapes can use the same shot discipline; the two EP11 examples show distinct cases.

## Build a readable story

State the premise and ending in one or two sentences. Work backward to the actions that make the ending understandable. Give every shot one story job: establish, set up, complicate, reveal, react, or resolve. An empty reaction can be useful, but a clip without a job need not stay in the cut. Give dialogue only to the beats that earn it; decide which character says each line before generation and leave room in the picture for that line. Verify the generated audio against that plan.

Lock identity and geography before video rounds: character appearance, clothing, important props, relationships, place, light, and screen direction. Repeat the necessary anchors in each shot request. For miniature stories, put familiar objects in the image to show scale instead of relying on “tiny” in text. For ensemble stories, check cast, setting, and prop positions at every scene change. The editor may shorten a generated clip to its single useful beat; source length is not the story's pacing.

Try the highest-risk shots first. For a silent gag, test whether the scale and physical interaction can be read, including the final visual punchline. For an ensemble scene, test a character interaction or spoken moment and listen for unexpected dialogue. If the full film has a large amount of generated material, preserve only the beats needed for understanding. A later still insert or local cut can be a legitimate repair if it reads on screen, but label it as post work.

## Audio and continuity in the cut

Watch the handoff of props and character gaze. A reaction to an object that appears between cuts without its cause will feel like a jump, even if both images are individually good. Check each dialogue line for duplicates, voice identity and mouth timing. Diagnose a reported sound defect before applying denoise or reverb removal. When replacing a generated voice with TTS or another take, disclose the source and listen to the combined result. Inspect environmental sound at the same cut points as picture; abrupt source loudness or a silent insert can expose the edit.
