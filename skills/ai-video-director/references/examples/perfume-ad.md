# Example: OpenCraft perfume product ad

**Form:** product ad. **Production:** EP11, October 18–19, 2026. **Tools actually used:** Meta Muse for image and video generations; Claude for planning, selection, local checks and edits; ChatCut for the working timeline, wordmark and music. This is a case, not a current Muse specification or a reusable speed preset.

## Brief and locked product

The creator wanted a striking ad for an OpenCraft perfume. They chose the dark eclipse idea, then selected bottle variant **BOT-D** despite the agent's recommendation of another variant. BOT-D's flat round flask, glossy black crescent, amber liquid, gold arc and cap became the product reference. The first silhouette still hid the liquid and looked empty. The creator rejected it; the accepted replacement, **KF-P1a**, kept a dim amber fill line visible. The useful rule is to check product truth in the darkest shot, not just visual consistency.

## What failed and what changed

The first four-shot cut used separate rim light, strip light, amber macro and backlit haze. Each shot looked plausible alone; the creator rejected the sequence for poor continuity. A comparison of perfume product films led to a shared home pose, one dark studio and one warm light progression. An amber ripple opener was then proposed as a shape match to the flask. Its ellipses required a measured correction, but the creator still rejected that opener: the liquid plane lacked the bottle's material and world. The shape match solved geometry without solving the ad.

The creator kept the **D2-B** light-reveal take. The rebuilt structure used a macro of the flask's gold arc, the long D2 reveal, short detail inserts, then a pull-back and rising mist. The pull-back began from the *last frame used* in D2, retaining flask position and light state. The wordmark was placed in the dark floor area of the product shot and delayed until the bottle had moved clear. The creator preferred the fast-cut version. A three-beat eclipse opener was tried; the first eclipse clip and two weak inserts were removed after review. A new close-up ripple clip worked only when the close start frame was tested without the wider bottle reference; that diagnosis came from this run and should be retested with another model.

The music was generated with the creator's specific authorization. Its actual onsets, rather than the requested prompt timing, were used to align cuts. The creator approved the music and beat placement by ear. A later rotating-flask mist take, **R3-B**, was accepted for the ending. The recorded working timeline was about 16 seconds at that point.

## Reusable decisions and limits

- Lock what is physically distinctive about the product before making the rest of the shots; show those traits in each relevant frame.
- Use a small set of visual relationships across the film: same bottle, studio, light travel and return to a recognizable hero pose. Reject an attractive shot if it belongs to a different visual world.
- Inspect the exact outgoing and incoming frames. The final-frame handoff fixed the D2 → pull-back join more directly than a transition effect.
- Time a brand mark after the product settles and reserve space to read it. Fit cuts to the real soundtrack, then listen.

**Verification boundary:** The creator approved the music/cut and rotating ending in the working ChatCut version. Source clips and sampled timeline frames were checked; the run record did not establish a standalone export or a complete final listen after the rotating-shot replacement.
